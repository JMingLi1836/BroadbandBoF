# Charter for Broadband

---

## 1. Overview

---

Broadband networks are evolving to support increasingly diverse services and applications, including traditional Internet access, real-time interactive services, cloud services, and emerging AI-related services. These services have different requirements for bandwidth, latency, reliability, security, and service assurance.

Existing broadband access architectures and protocols, many of which were developed decades ago, face challenges in supporting these evolving requirements. Operators have introduced various vendor-specific extensions and mechanisms to address operational needs, resulting in fragmentation and interoperability issues. Meanwhile, broadband-related standardization activities are distributed across multiple IETF working groups, without a dedicated place to coordinate common requirements and protocol evolution.

The Broadband Working Group (WG) will provide a dedicated place to address broadband networking requirements, identify gaps in existing protocols and architectures, and develop solutions to support the evolution of broadband networks.

## 2. Scope

---

The Broadband WG will focus on the evolution of broadband access networks and related service delivery mechanisms. Its scope includes:

- **Use Cases and Requirements:** Identify broadband use cases and operational requirements from network operators, covering access, subscriber and service management, service differentiation, and service assurance.

- **Protocol Gap Analysis and Evolution:** Analyze limitations of existing broadband protocols and architectures, such as PPPoE, IPoE-based access, and ANCP, and identify requirements for their evolution.

- **Protocol Extensions and New Mechanisms:** Develop broadband-specific protocol extensions and mechanisms to address identified gaps, particularly those related to access management, service identification, authentication, policy control, and differentiated service delivery.

- **Operational and Deployment Considerations:** Address interoperability, scalability, security, manageability, and deployment considerations for broadband networks.

The WG will reuse existing protocols and mechanisms wherever possible. For protocols that are maintained by other IETF working groups, the Broadband WG will coordinate with the responsible groups to address broadband-specific requirements and extensions.

The WG will not duplicate the responsibilities of existing working groups or undertake protocol work unrelated to broadband networking.

## 3. Work Items

---

The Broadband WG is expected to produce the following deliverables:

- **Problem Statement, Use Cases, Requirements, and Gap Analysis:** Document broadband networking use cases, operational requirements, and limitations of existing protocols and architectures. (Informational)

- **Broadband Architecture and Framework:** Define a framework for broadband network evolution, including access, subscriber and service management, and differentiated service delivery. (optional, Informational)

- **Protocol Specifications:** Develop one or more protocol specifications for broadband-specific extensions or new mechanisms based on the identified requirements. Protocol work will focus on areas that are not adequately addressed by existing IETF working groups. (Standards Track, as appropriate)

- **Operational and Deployment Guidance:** Document deployment considerations, interoperability requirements, and operational recommendations for broadband networks. (Informational)

- **Management and Data Models:** Develop management or data models where necessary to support the configuration, monitoring, and operation of mechanisms defined by the WG. (As appropriate)

The WG will prioritize the problem statement, requirements, and gap analysis before proceeding with protocol specifications and other technical solutions.

## 4. Collaborations

---

The Broadband WG will coordinate with relevant IETF working groups and other standardization bodies to ensure consistency with existing protocols and avoid duplication of work. Key collaborations include, but are not limited to:

- DHC and RADIUS: Coordinate on broadband access, subscriber authentication, and related protocol requirements and extensions.

- OPS and Network Management WGs: Coordinate on network operations, management, observability, and service assurance.

- Other relevant IETF WGs: Collaborate with relevant working groups in the Internet, Routing, and Transport areas as needed.

The WG will also coordinate with external standards organizations, including the Broadband Forum (BBF), to align requirements and protocol status and avoid overlap or conflicts of standardizations.
